What should you look for in an alcohol tracker?

What should you look for in an alcohol tracker?

A good alcohol tracker should be fast enough to use in real life, detailed enough to produce useful analysis, and transparent enough that your own data does not get trapped behind an account or paywall.

Criteria to compare

CriterionWhy it matters
Fast drink loggingIf logging takes too long, the record will be incomplete.
Alcohol-specific unitsA drink count misses ABV, serving size, and cocktails.
Useful analyticsThe app should show weekly totals, dry days, heavy sessions, and trends over time.
Export optionsYour history should be portable for backup or your own analysis.
Clear privacy modelYou should know whether data is local, synced, shared, or tied to an account.
Fair paywallBe careful when an app collects your data for free but charges to reveal basic insights from it.

What useful analysis looks like

Useful analysis does more than count drinks. It shows whether your intake is spread across the week or concentrated in one session, whether dry days are increasing, and whether changes actually reduce total units over time.

How to choose

Choose the tracker you will still use after a normal night out. Then check the data terms: can you export your history, see meaningful analytics, and understand the pricing before your own insights are locked away?

FAQ

Do I need a no-account alcohol tracker?

Not always. Accounts can help with sync, but they should not be required just to understand your own drinking pattern.

Should a tracker count units?

Yes. Units help compare beer, wine, spirits, cocktails, and different serving sizes fairly.

What analytics matter most?

Weekly totals, dry days, heavier sessions, trends over time, and changes after goals are more useful than a raw drink count.

Should I pay for alcohol tracking?

Paying for a good tool can be fair. Be cautious when basic analysis or data export is hidden after you have already entered your history.

How does privacy fit in?

Privacy is one criterion. It matters because honest logging is easier when you understand where your data lives and how it can be exported or deleted.
